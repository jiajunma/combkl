# 3. Dihedral intervals and transported reflection orders

An interval isomorphism initially preserves only order. This chapter extracts the rank-two information needed to compare local images without assuming that root labels have been matched.

## Relative intervals

Start with [[node:kl.notes.relative_dihedral_intervals]]. The dihedral subgroup has its own Coxeter generators, order, and length. Relative paths are ambient paths; the converse identification of partial orders is not assumed.

## Recovering and transporting the graph

[[node:kl.paths.graph_invariance]] reconstructs every directed reflection edge from the interval order. [[node:kl.notes.relative_dihedral_transport]] then transports an entire relative interval, including its rank differences and range, to a target root plane. Preserving a diagrammatic shape alone would not supply this range statement.

## From edge counts to polynomial degree

[[node:kl.paths.later_count]] computes the excess of later outgoing conditions at the two endpoints of an edge. Its \(A_2\) example is a useful check on the role of a long reflection edge.

[[node:kl.paths.plane_degree]] sums the planar contributions. Together, these two results provide the local vanishing orders and the global degree of the correction factor that appears in Chapter 5.
