# 1. Introduction and conventions

The question is whether an unlabelled Bruhat interval determines its ordinary equal-parameter Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomial. Start with [[node:kl.main.combinatorial_invariance]] as the goal. The rest of this chapter fixes the objects and conventions that occur in that statement.

## Bruhat order and the information retained by an interval

Read [[node:kl.foundations.bruhat_step]], [[node:kl.foundations.bruhat_order]], and [[node:kl.foundations.interval]] in that order. The distinction between reflection edges and covers is important: an interval gives its order immediately, but later arguments need the full directed reflection graph. The type \(A_2\) example illustrates what has to be reconstructed.

## Hecke operators and canonical polynomials

[[node:kl.notes.hecke_action]] develops the operators, their quadratic relation, and the bar involution. Then [[node:kl.foundations.normalized_kl]] states the precise R/P conditions. Work through its rank-one calculation before continuing to [[node:kl.foundations.low_half]] and [[node:kl.foundations.canonical_kl]].

There are two polynomial parameters. The classical variable is \(q\); the real comparison uses \(q=t^2\). [[node:kl.foundations.rtilde]] explains the normalized R-polynomial, and [[node:kl.foundations.scaled_kl]] derives the reciprocal vector formula with coefficient \(t^{-1}-t\).

These constructions use Coxeter-system data. The theorem to be proved says that the final polynomial forgets that extra data and remembers the abstract interval.
