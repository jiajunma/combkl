# Ideals generated by linear subspaces

Let \(k\) be a field and \(V\) a \(k\)-vector space. Its symmetric algebra \(A=\operatorname{Sym}_k(V)\) is generated by a linear map \(\iota:V\to A\), with the property that every linear map from \(V\) into a commutative \(k\)-algebra extends uniquely to an algebra homomorphism from \(A\).

Choosing a basis of \(V\) identifies its vectors with degree-one polynomial generators. An arbitrary vector becomes its linear coordinate polynomial.

For a subspace \(P\subseteq V\), define the linear ideal
\[
I_P=(\iota(v):v\in P)\subseteq A.
\]
An element of this ideal is a finite sum \(\sum_j a_j\iota(v_j)\), with \(a_j\in A\) and \(v_j\in P\).

## The map induced by a vector-space quotient

The quotient map \(\pi:V\to V/P\) gives a linear map
\(v\mapsto\iota_{V/P}(\pi(v))\).
Its extension to symmetric algebras is
\[
Q_P:\operatorname{Sym}_k(V)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sym}_k(V/P),
\qquad Q_P(\iota(v))=\iota_{V/P}([v]).
\]
Every generator from \(P\) goes to zero. Thus this map factors through the quotient \(A/I_P\). The [[node:kl.detail.linear_quotient|linear-quotient theorem]] constructs its inverse.

## Prime ideals, prime elements and units

A unit is an element with a two-sided multiplicative inverse. A prime ideal is a proper ideal \(J\) such that \(xy\in J\) implies \(x\in J\) or \(y\in J\).

In a domain, a prime element is a nonzero nonunit \(p\) such that \(p\mid xy\) implies \(p\mid x\) or \(p\mid y\). Its principal ideal is prime. Two scalars are relatively prime when every common divisor is a unit. They are associated when one is a unit multiple of the other.

These notions describe linear root labels: the quotient theorem gives prime linear ideals, and divisibility between degree-one generators is determined by their vector lines.

## A root line and a root ideal

For one vector \(a\in V\), the root line and root ideal are
\[
ka=\operatorname{span}_k\{a\},\qquad
(\iota(a))\subseteq A.
\]
The source names them rootLine and rootIdeal. The ideal generated by the whole line is the same principal ideal, because \(\iota(ca)=c\,\iota(a)\).

Reduction of another vector \(v\) means its actual class \([\iota(v)]\) in \(A/(\iota(a))\).

For \(V=k e_1\oplus k e_2\), write \(A=k[X,Y]\). The line \(ke_1\) gives ideal \((X)\), and reduction leaves the polynomial variable \(Y\). The line \(k(e_1+e_2)\) gives ideal \((X+Y)\); in the quotient, \(X=-Y\).
