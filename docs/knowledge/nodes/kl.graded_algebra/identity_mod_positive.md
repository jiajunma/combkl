# A graded map equal to the identity modulo the positive ideal is invertible

Let \(A\) be a commutative graded Noetherian algebra, and let \(M\) be a finite \(A\)-module with a compatible nonnegative grading. Write \(J=A_{>0}\) for the ideal generated by positive-degree scalars.

Suppose \(f:M\to M\) preserves degree and
\[
m-f(m)\in JM\qquad(m\in M).
\]
Then \(f\) is bijective. Equivalently, a degree-zero endomorphism inducing the identity on \(M/JM\) is an automorphism.

*Proof.*

### Step 1. Express every vector using the image and positive multiples

Let \(N=\operatorname{im}f\). Every \(m\in M\) has the expression
\[
m=f(m)+(m-f(m)),
\]
whose two summands belong to \(N\) and \(JM\), respectively. Thus \(M=N+JM\).

The image \(N\) is homogeneous: the degree-\(n\) component of \(f(m)\) equals \(f(m_n)\), again in the image.

### Step 2. Apply graded Nakayama to the image

The module is nonnegative, \(N\) is homogeneous, and \(M=N+JM\). These are the hypotheses of [[node:kl.notes.graded_nakayama|graded Nakayama]] in its submodule form. It gives \(N=M\), proving surjectivity of \(f\).

The degree descent behind that application expresses each missing homogeneous vector through positive-degree coefficients multiplying vectors of strictly smaller degree; the nonnegative bound starts the induction.

### Step 3. Stabilize the kernels of successive powers

A finite module over a Noetherian ring is a Noetherian module: its ascending chains of submodules stabilize. Consider
\[
\ker f^0\subseteq\ker f^1\subseteq\ker f^2\subseteq\cdots,
\]
where \(f^0\) is the identity. Choose \(r\) with
\(\ker f^r=\ker f^{r+1}\).

Let \(m\in\ker f\). Since \(f\) is surjective, its power \(f^r\) is surjective. Choose \(v\) with \(f^r(v)=m\). Then
\[
f^{r+1}(v)=f(m)=0.
\]
The stabilized-kernel equality puts \(v\) in \(\ker f^r\), so \(m=f^r(v)=0\). Thus \(\ker f=0\), proving injectivity.

### Step 4. Preserve the grading of the inverse

For \(y\in M_n\), let \(m=f^{-1}(y)\) and decompose \(m=\sum_jm_j\). The components \(f(m_j)\) lie in degree \(j\) and sum to \(y\). For \(j\ne n\), uniqueness of components gives \(f(m_j)=0\). Injectivity makes \(m_j=0\). Hence \(f^{-1}(y)=m_n\in M_n\), so the inverse is degree zero as well.
