# Finite products and shifted free modules

Finite graded products are formed one component at a time. For modules \((M_i)_{i\in I}\) with \(I\) finite, set
\[
M=\prod_{i\in I}M_i,\qquad M_n=\prod_{i\in I}(M_i)_n.
\]
A finite product is also the finite direct sum: a vector is a tuple, and coordinate insertion expresses it as the sum of its individual coordinates.

The projection to \(M_i\) preserves degree. A family of degree-zero maps \(f_i:M_i\to N_i\) gives the degree-zero tuple map \((m_i)\mapsto(f_i(m_i))\). The binary case is \(M\oplus N\), with degree-\(n\) piece \(M_n\times N_n\).

## Zero and unit module data

The zero module has one vector and zero graded pieces in every degree. Its only vector is the empty homogeneous sum, and it is generated by the empty family.

For a nonnegatively graded coefficient algebra \(A\), the unit module is the rank-one free \(A\)-module with generator in degree zero. The source represents it as functions from a one-element index type to \(A\), which identify with \(A\) by evaluation.

## A shifted free module with integer generator degrees

For a finite set \(I\) and integers \(d_i\), take the free module \(F=A^I\), with basis \(e_i\). Define
\[
F_n=\{(a_i):a_i\in A_{n-d_i}\text{ for every }i\}.
\]
Then \(e_i\) has degree \(d_i\), because its nonzero coefficient is \(1\in A_0\).

The component of a tuple \(a=(a_i)\) in degree \(n\) is
\[
(\pi_n^F(a))_i=\pi_{n-d_i}^A(a_i).
\]
For each coordinate, the decomposition of \(a_i\) has finite support. Since \(I\) is finite, the union of these supports after translating by \(d_i\) is finite. Thus the tuple has a finite homogeneous decomposition.

If \(A\) is nonnegative, \(F_n=0\) whenever \(n<d_i\) for every \(i\). In particular \(d_i\ge0\) for all \(i\) makes \(F\) nonnegative; this is the input required by ModuleData.free.

The general shifted-piece construction allows arbitrary integer \(d_i\). A generator of degree \(-2\), for example, contributes a shifted copy whose first nonzero piece is in degree \(-2\). Such integer shifts are used in the general Hilbert-series arguments.

## Decomposition of a finite product

For arbitrary graded \(M_i\), the same coordinate argument applies:
\[
\pi_n((m_i)_i)=(\pi_n(m_i))_i.
\]
The finite union of the coordinate supports supplies existence of decomposition; uniqueness follows by looking at each coordinate. Scalar multiplication respects degree coordinatewise, and the union of finite generating families generates the product.

For \(A=k[X]\), generator degrees \(0,2\) give \(F_n=A_n\oplus A_{n-2}\), the running example \(A\oplus A(2)\).
