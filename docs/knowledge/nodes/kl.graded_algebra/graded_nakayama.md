# Graded Nakayama by induction on degree

Let \(A\) be nonnegatively graded, and let \(M\) be a graded module bounded below. Let \(N\subseteq M\) be closed under homogeneous components. If
\[
N+A_{>0}M=M,
\]
then \(N=M\).

*Proof.*

### Step 1. Reformulate what must be proved

Let \(J=A_{>0}\), let \(N\subseteq M\) be closed under homogeneous components, and assume \(M=N+JM\). Suppose \(M_n=0\) for \(n<d_0\). It suffices to show that every homogeneous element lies in \(N\), because each element of a graded direct sum has only finitely many nonzero homogeneous components.

Use strong induction on the nonnegative integer \(n-d_0\). Degrees below \(d_0\) contribute only zero.

### Step 2. Examine a positive-degree product in degree n

Take homogeneous \(a\in A_r\) with \(r>0\) and an arbitrary \(m\in M\). Decompose \(m=\sum_jm_j\). The degree-\(n\) component of \(am\) is \(am_{n-r}\), since \(am_j\) has degree \(r+j\).

The index \(n-r\) is strictly smaller than \(n\). If it is below \(d_0\), the component is zero. Otherwise the induction hypothesis puts \(m_{n-r}\) in \(N\), and closure under scalar multiplication puts \(am_{n-r}\) in \(N\).

Since \(J\) is generated by positive-degree homogeneous elements, additivity extends this conclusion to every element of \(JM\): its degree-\(n\) component lies in \(N\).

### Step 3. Use the generation hypothesis

Take \(v\in M_n\). Write \(v=u+w\) with \(u\in N\) and \(w\in JM\), using \(M=N+JM\). Project to degree \(n\):
\[
v=u_n+w_n.
\]
The first term belongs to \(N\) by its homogeneous-component closure. The second belongs to \(N\) by Step 2. Hence \(v\in N\), proving the induction step.

### Step 4. Reassemble all degrees

Every \(m\in M\) is a finite sum of homogeneous components, all now in \(N\). Thus \(M=N\). In particular, taking \(N=0\), the identity \(M=JM\) forces \(M=0\).

The lower bound is the reason the induction starts. Without it, repeated division into lower degrees could continue indefinitely and this conclusion would not follow.
