# Linear ideals, symmetric-algebra quotients, and primality

Let \(V\) be a vector space over a field \(k\), \(P\subseteq V\) a linear subspace, and \(I_P\subseteq\operatorname{Sym}_k(V)\) the ideal generated by \(P\). There is a canonical isomorphism
\[
\operatorname{Sym}_k(V)/I_P\simeq\operatorname{Sym}_k(V/P).
\]
The ideal \(I_P\) is prime, and \(\iota(v)\in I_P\) exactly when \(v\in P\). For \(P=ka\), this gives \(A/(a)\simeq\operatorname{Sym}_k(V/ka)\).

*Proof.*

### Step 1. Construct the forward map

Compose the quotient map \(V\to V/P\) with the degree-one inclusion into \(\operatorname{Sym}(V/P)\). The universal property of the symmetric algebra extends it to an algebra map \(Q:\operatorname{Sym}(V)\to\operatorname{Sym}(V/P)\). Every generator \(\iota(p)\), \(p\in P\), maps to zero. Hence \(I_P\subseteq\ker Q\), and \(Q\) factors through the quotient by \(I_P\).

### Step 2. Construct its inverse

The map \(v\mapsto[\iota(v)]\) into \(\operatorname{Sym}(V)/I_P\) kills \(P\). It therefore descends to a linear map on \(V/P\). Extend this map to the symmetric algebra of \(V/P\). On each degree-one generator, both composites are the identity. Since degree-one generators generate the symmetric algebra, the composites are identity algebra maps. This proves the isomorphism and \(I_P=\ker Q\).

### Step 3. Detect degree-one membership

If \(v\in P\), the element \(\iota(v)\) is one of the ideal's generators. Conversely, if \(\iota(v)\in I_P\), Step 1 gives \(\iota([v])=0\) in \(\operatorname{Sym}(V/P)\). If \([v]\ne0\), choose a linear functional \(\lambda:V/P\to k\) with \(\lambda([v])\ne0\). Its extension to the symmetric algebra sends \(\iota([v])\) to that nonzero scalar, a contradiction. Therefore \([v]=0\) and \(v\in P\).

### Step 4. Deduce primality and the localization criterion

The symmetric algebra of a vector space over a field is a polynomial algebra after choosing a basis, and is an integral domain. Thus the quotient from Step 2 is a domain and \(I_P\) is prime.

Transport the ideal through the chosen polynomial-coordinate isomorphism for the extended realization. Injectivity of the root-space embedding preserves the membership criterion in Step 3. In the localization at this prime, an element of the coefficient ring becomes a unit exactly when it lies outside the prime. Applied to a root label, this says that the localized label is a unit exactly when its root lies outside the selected plane.
