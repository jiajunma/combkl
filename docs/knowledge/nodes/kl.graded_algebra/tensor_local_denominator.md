# Clearing a denominator in an embedded tensor localization

Let \(R\) be a commutative ring, \(P\subseteq R\) a multiplicative submonoid, \(S=P^{-1}R\) a localization, and \(M\) an \(R\)-module. For a submodule \(L\subseteq M\), let
\[
L_S=\operatorname{im}(S\otimes_RL\longrightarrow S\otimes_RM)
\]
be its embedded scalar extension.

The submodule \(L_S\) is the localized submodule represented by numerators in \(L\). In particular, if \(x\in M\) satisfies
\[
1\otimes x\in L_S,
\]
there is \(d\in P\) such that \(dx\in L\).

*Proof.*

### Step 1. Identify embedded tensors with localized submodule elements

The scalar extension \(L_S\) is the \(S\)-span of the images \(1\otimes m\), for \(m\in L\). The localized submodule is generated by those same images: a fraction \(m/s\), \(m\in L\), equals
\[
(s/1)^{-1}(1\otimes m)
\]
under the usual tensor description of the localized module. Conversely, for a finite sum of scalar multiples, write each scalar as \(r_i/s_i\), with \(s_i\in P\). Under the tensor-to-fraction description it becomes
\[
\sum_i(r_i/s_i)\otimes m_i
=
\frac{\sum_i r_i(\prod_{j\ne i}s_j)m_i}{\prod_i s_i}.
\]
The numerator belongs to \(L\) and the denominator belongs to \(P\). Thus every element of the span has a numerator in \(L\).

Thus the two constructions describe the same submodule of \(S\otimes_RM\). This is the source's `baseChange_eq_localized` identity.

### Step 2. Choose a numerator in the original submodule

By Step 1, write the given localized element as
\[
x/1=m/s,\qquad m\in L,\quad s\in P.
\]
Equivalently, the images of \(m\) and \(sx\) in the localized module agree.

The definition of equality of module fractions supplies another element \(t\in P\) such that
\[
t(m-sx)=0\quad\text{in }M.
\]
Therefore \(tm=(ts)x\) in the original module.

### Step 3. Multiply the two denominator factors

Set \(d=ts\). The submonoid is closed under multiplication, so \(d\in P\). Because \(m\in L\) and \(L\) is an \(R\)-submodule, \(tm\in L\). Hence
\[
dx=(ts)x=tm\in L,
\]
which is the required original-module membership.

For a prime localization \(S=R_{\mathfrak p}\), the conclusion reads \(d\notin\mathfrak p\) and \(dx\in L\). The two factors \(s,t\) respectively represent the fraction and clear its equality in the module.
