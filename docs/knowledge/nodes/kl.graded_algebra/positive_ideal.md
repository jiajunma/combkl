# The positive ideal and degree-zero augmentation

Let \(k\) be a field and \(A\) a commutative \(k\)-algebra with an integer grading \(A=\bigoplus_nA_n\). Its positive ideal is
\[
A_+=\bigl(a:a\in A_n\text{ for some }n>0\bigr).
\]
The parentheses denote the ideal generated by those elements. Its members are finite sums \(\sum_j r_ja_j\), where \(r_j\in A\) and each \(a_j\) is homogeneous of positive degree.

Assume now that \(A_n=0\) for \(n<0\). The source defines the augmentation as
\[
\epsilon:A\longrightarrow A,\qquad \epsilon(a)=\pi_0(a).
\]
Its codomain in Lean is \(A\), and its image is exactly \(A_0\): every degree-zero element is fixed by the projection. The [[node:kl.algebra.augmentation_kernel|augmentation theorem]] proves that it is a ring homomorphism and that
\[
\ker\epsilon=A_+=\bigoplus_{n>0}A_n.
\]

## Connected coefficient algebras

The grading is connected when every degree-zero element is the image of a scalar in \(k\). If \(A\) is nonzero, the algebra map embeds the field \(k\), so it identifies \(A_0\) with \(k\). Under this identification the augmentation is a map \(A\to k\), and
\[
A/A_+\simeq k.
\]
The maps send \([a]\) to the scalar representing \(\pi_0(a)\), and a scalar \(c\) to \([c\,1_A]\). They are inverse because \(a-\pi_0(a)\in A_+\), while degree-zero projection fixes constants.

Every \(a\in A\) is then congruent modulo \(A_+\) to its degree-zero scalar.

For \(A=k[X_1,\ldots,X_N]\) with variables of degree one,
\[
A_+=(X_1,\ldots,X_N),\qquad
\epsilon(f)=\text{the constant polynomial }f(0,\ldots,0).
\]
Thus \(X+Y^2\) has augmentation zero and \(1+X\) has augmentation one.

## Reduction of a module

For an \(A\)-module \(M\), the submodule \(A_+M\) consists of finite sums of products \(am\), \(a\in A_+\). The quotient \(M/A_+M\) is a module over \(A/A_+\), hence a \(k\)-vector space in the nonzero connected case.

If \(M=Ae_0\oplus Ae_2\) with generator degrees zero and two, its reduction has basis \(\overline e_0,\overline e_2\) over \(k\). Polynomial multiples of positive degree vanish in the quotient, leaving the homogeneous generators and their degrees.

A minimal graded free cover has kernel contained in the positive ideal times its source. Its reduced generators form a basis of this quotient; graded Nakayama lifts that information to generation in the original module.
