# The positive ideal and augmentation quotient

Let \(A=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb Z}A_n\) be a graded commutative \(k\)-algebra. Its positive ideal is
\[
A_+=\bigl(a:a\in A_n\text{ for some }n>0\bigr).
\]
The parentheses mean the ideal generated by these elements: an element of \(A_+\) is a finite sum \(\sum_j r_ja_j\), with \(r_j\in A\) and each \(a_j\) homogeneous of positive degree.

## A nonnegative grading

Assume \(A_n=0\) for \(n<0\). Decompose each coefficient \(r_j\) into homogeneous components. Multiplication by a positive-degree \(a_j\) then gives only positive degrees. Conversely, every positive homogeneous element is a generator of \(A_+\). Hence
\[
A_+=\bigoplus_{n>0}A_n.
\]

A nonnegative grading is connected when \(A_0\) is exactly the image of the base field \(k\). For a nonzero \(k\)-algebra, this identifies \(A_0\) with \(k\).

In this connected case, projection to degree zero is an algebra homomorphism
\[
\epsilon:A\to k.
\]
Indeed, the only way two nonnegative degrees can add to zero is for both to be zero, so \((ab)_0=a_0b_0\). The map fixes constants, and its kernel is \(A_+\). Consequently \(A/A_+\simeq k\).

For a polynomial ring with variables of degree one,
\[
A_+=(X_1,\ldots,X_N),\qquad \epsilon(f)=f(0,\ldots,0).
\]
For instance, \(X+Y^2\) lies in the positive ideal, while \(1+X\) has augmentation one.

## Reduction of a module

For an \(A\)-module \(M\), the submodule \(A_+M\) consists of finite sums \(am\) with \(a\in A_+\), \(m\in M\). The quotient \(M/A_+M\) is a vector space over \(A/A_+\), and thus over \(k\) in the connected case \(A_0=k\).

If \(M=Ae_0\oplus Ae_2\), with generator degrees zero and two, its reduction has basis \(\overline e_0,\overline e_2\) over \(k\). Positive-degree polynomial multiples disappear; the generator degrees remain recorded by the induced grading.

A minimal graded cover \(F\to M\) has kernel contained in \(A_+F\). Its generators consequently become a basis of \(M/A_+M\). [[node:kl.notes.graded_nakayama|Graded Nakayama]] lifts this reduced information back to a generating family in \(M\).
