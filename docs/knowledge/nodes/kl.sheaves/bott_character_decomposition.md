# Simultaneous decomposition of Bott–Samelson stalk and kernel characters

Fix a reduced word with product \(b\). There are polynomials \(m_y(q)\in\mathbb Z[q]\) with nonnegative coefficients such that the associated Bott–Samelson stalk characters and full upward-kernel characters decompose simultaneously as
\[
C^{\mathrm{word}}_x(q)=\sum_{y\le b}m_y(q)C_{x,y}(q),
\]
\[
K^{\mathrm{word}}_x(q)
=\sum_{y\le b}m_y(q)\,q^{d(x,y)}C_{x,y}(q^{-1}).
\]
Character terms outside their lower intervals are interpreted as zero.

*Proof.*

### Step 1. Verify the model-decomposition inputs for the word sheaf

Choose a reduced word \(\mathbf s\) with product \(b\), and take its actual polynomial-coordinate word sheaf. Its stalks are finite graded free, its upper edge maps are the label quotients, it is globally generated by word-module sections, and its flabbiness is proved in [[node:kl.detail.word_flabbiness]].

The family of boundary models \(O_y\), \(y\le b\), has the same quotient rule and normalized top stalks. Thus the graded sheaf decomposition theorem applies:
\[
B^{\mathbf s}\simeq\bigoplus_{y,i}O_y(d_{y,i}),
\qquad d_{y,i}\ge0.
\]
This is a single sheaf isomorphism, with all restriction maps included.

### Step 2. Group the shifts into multiplicity polynomials

Define \(m_y(q)=\sum_iq^{d_{y,i}}\). The index sets are finite, so these are integral polynomials. Their coefficients count basis shifts and are therefore nonnegative.

At a vertex \(x\), the sheaf isomorphism gives a graded module isomorphism
\(B^{\mathbf s,x}\simeq\bigoplus_{y,i}O_y^x(d_{y,i})\).
Characters are additive on finite direct sums and a shift multiplies the character by \(q^{d_{y,i}}\). Hence
\[
C_x^{\mathrm{word}}=\sum_y m_yC_{x,y}.
\]
Models with \(x\not\le y\) contribute zero by their support condition.

### Step 3. Apply the same isomorphism to outgoing kernels

The stalk and edge maps of the isomorphism commute with restrictions. A stalk vector has every outgoing restriction zero if and only if its image has every corresponding restriction zero. The full outgoing kernel therefore decomposes using the same summands \(O_y(d_{y,i})\).

Kernels of a finite direct sum of componentwise maps are the direct sum of their kernels: the restriction of a tuple is zero exactly when each component restriction is zero. Thus their characters add with the same multiplicities \(m_y\).

### Step 4. Substitute each model's kernel character

For \(O_y\), stalk–costalk reciprocity identifies the kernel character at \(x\) with
\(\operatorname{reflect}_{d(x,y)}C_{x,y}\).
Equivalently it is \(q^{d(x,y)}C_{x,y}(q^{-1})\), with the weak degree bound furnished by the dual kernel grading. Shifting the model multiplies this character by the same \(q^{d_{y,i}}\) as in Step 2.

Summing gives the second displayed decomposition. There is no independent choice of multiplicities for kernels: both formulas come from the original sheaf isomorphism.

### Step 5. Check the top multiplicity

At \(x=b\), every model with \(y<b\) has zero stalk. The remaining model \(O_b\) has character one, and the reduced-word top stalk also has character one. The first decomposition therefore reads \(1=m_b\). This is the coefficient used in the subsequent cancellation argument.
