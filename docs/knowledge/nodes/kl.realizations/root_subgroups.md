# Reflection-generated and displacement subgroups of a root subspace

Let \(\rho:W\to\operatorname{GL}(V_0)\) be the geometric action on the original root space, and let \(P\subseteq V_0\) be any linear subspace.

The displacement subgroup is
\[
G_P=\{w\in W:\rho(w)v-v\in P\text{ for every }v\in V_0\}.
\]
Equivalently, the image of \(\rho(w)-1\) is contained in \(P\). Such an element preserves \(P\) and induces the identity on \(V_0/P\).

The reflection-generated subgroup is
\[
D_P=\left\langle r_a:a\text{ is a root and }a\in P\right\rangle.
\]
Here \(r_a(v)=v-B(a,v)a\). The brackets denote the smallest subgroup containing the indicated reflections, hence all finite products of them and their inverses.

Since \(a\) and \(-a\) define the same reflection and a subspace is closed under negation, the same subgroup is generated by the positive roots in \(P\). The source calls this subgroup the plane subgroup; its definition applies to arbitrary subspaces as well as root planes.

## The subgroup laws for displacement

The identity has zero displacement. If \(w,z\in G_P\), then
\[
\rho(wz)v-v
=\bigl(\rho(w)(\rho(z)v)-\rho(z)v\bigr)
 +\bigl(\rho(z)v-v\bigr).
\]
The first term lies in \(P\) by the condition on \(w\), applied to \(\rho(z)v\); the second lies in \(P\) by the condition on \(z\). Their sum lies in \(P\).

For \(w\in G_P\), apply its condition to \(\rho(w^{-1})v\):
\[
v-\rho(w^{-1})v\in P.
\]
Negating gives the inverse condition. Thus \(G_P\) is a subgroup.

For \(a\in P\), write
\(\rho(w)a=(\rho(w)a-a)+a\).
Both terms lie in \(P\), proving preservation of the subspace. The defining displacement equation then says precisely that the induced quotient map is the identity.

The [[node:kl.realization.plane_reflection_membership|reflection-membership theorem]] proves \(D_P\subseteq G_P\) and identifies exactly which ambient reflections belong to \(D_P\).
