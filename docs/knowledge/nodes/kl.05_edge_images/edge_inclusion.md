# The edge-image bound from upper-kernel freeness

Fix an interval isomorphism between two finite-generator Coxeter systems and the transported edge schedule. At an edge \(e:x\to y\), let \(F,G\) be the remaining upward conditions at the endpoints, and put
\[
I_e=\rho_{x,e}L_x(F),\qquad J_e=\rho_{y,e}L_y(G).
\]
If \(L_y(G)\) is free, then in the common edge module
\[
I_e\subseteq f_eJ_e,\qquad
\deg f_e=\frac{\ell(y)-\ell(x)-1}{2}.
\]

*Proof.*

Decompose the localized sheaf into dihedral models. In the same edge component, both images are products of root factors. [[node:kl.paths.later_count]] computes their difference of vanishing orders, and [[node:kl.paths.plane_degree]] gives the total degree of \(f_e\).

Upper-kernel freeness supplies a free target image. Apply [[node:kl.local.free_target_localization]] to combine the root-plane membership conditions into the global inclusion. Finally, transport the ambient statement to the current schedule using the interval restriction and the correspondence of remaining conditions.

## Comparing the images in the same edge module

At a root-plane prime, choose the actual sheaf isomorphism to the sum of dihedral models. Its edge component is a single linear isomorphism carrying both \(I_e\) and \(J_e\) into model coordinates. In a model supported above the upper endpoint, the images are ideals generated by products of the remaining labels. Their valuations differ by the later-edge count.

Using one edge isomorphism for both images is essential. Knowing only that stalk ranks agree would not identify two embedded submodules of the edge module. The local decomposition supplies the restriction-map compatibility that makes the comparison meaningful.

The upper image is free by [[node:kl.notes.upper_image_free]]. Multiplication by the nonzero correction factor preserves its freeness, and its generic span is the whole edge module. Height-one membership can therefore be tested against \(f_eJ_e\), yielding \(I_e\subseteq f_eJ_e\) globally. The possible quotient is retained until the endpoint comparison proves that its Hilbert value is zero.
