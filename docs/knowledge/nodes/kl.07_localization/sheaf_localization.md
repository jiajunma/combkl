# Localizing the sheaf at a root plane

For a flat scalar extension, finite upward-kernel conditions and their edge images commute with base change. In particular, one may compute the kernel and its image first and then localize, or perform both constructions on the localized sheaf.

*Proof.*

Combine the finitely many restriction maps into one map to a finite product of edge modules. Its kernel is the upward kernel. Flatness preserves its defining exact sequence, and tensor product commutes with the finite product. For the edge images, use the exact sequence presenting an image as the source modulo its kernel. These two facts give the corresponding lower-image and upper-image identifications.

Now take the polynomial ring \(A\) and the prime \(\mathfrak d=(\alpha,\beta)\) of a root plane. If a root label \(\gamma\) lies outside that plane, its linear form is outside \(\mathfrak d\) and becomes a unit in \(A_{\mathfrak d}\). The localized edge module \(A_{\mathfrak d}/(\gamma)\) is zero. Only plane edges survive.

For a fixed edge labelled \(\alpha\), pass to \(C=A/(\alpha)\). The image of \(\mathfrak d\) is the height-one prime generated by \(\overline\beta\). Its local ring is a discrete valuation ring. Every surviving label independent of \(\alpha\) becomes a unit times \(\overline\beta\). Thus products of labels can be compared by their integral vanishing orders.
