# The edge-image bound from upper-kernel freeness

Fix an interval isomorphism between two finite-generator Coxeter systems and the transported edge schedule. At an edge \(e:x\to y\), let \(F,G\) be the remaining upward conditions at the endpoints, and put
\[
I_e=\rho_{x,e}L_x(F),\qquad J_e=\rho_{y,e}L_y(G).
\]
If \(L_y(G)\) is free, then in the common edge module
\[
I_e\subseteq f_eJ_e,\qquad
\deg f_e=\frac{\ell(y)-\ell(x)-1}{2}.
\]

*Proof.*

### Step 1. Fix the ambient module and the proposed free target

For \(e:x\to y\), let \(\alpha=\alpha_e\), \(C=A/(\alpha)\), and
\[
I=\rho_{x,e}K_x(F),\qquad J=\rho_{y,e}K_y(G),\qquad S=f_eJ\subseteq B^e.
\]
The edge module is finite free over \(C\). Upper-kernel freeness and saturation give freeness of \(J\) over \(C\) by [[node:kl.notes.upper_image_free]]. The edge factor is nonzero in the polynomial domain \(C\), so multiplication by it identifies \(J\) with \(S\); thus \(S\) is free.

We will prove that an arbitrary \(z\in I\) belongs to \(S\).

### Step 2. Supply one explicit global denominator

Put \(g=\prod_{h\in G}\alpha_h\). Its reduction in \(C\) is nonzero, because no outgoing label at \(y\) is proportional to the incoming label \(\alpha\).
For any \(z\in B^e\), choose an upper-stalk lift \(v\in B^y\). Every outgoing restriction of \(gv\) indexed by \(G\) vanishes, since its own label is a factor of \(g\). Hence \(gz\in J\), and
\[
(f_eg)z\in f_eJ=S.
\]
Both factors remain nonzero in \(C\), so \(f_eg\) is a valid generic denominator.

### Step 3. Compare images at a root-plane prime

Let \(\beta\) be a root not proportional to \(\alpha\), and localize at the plane prime generated by \(\alpha,\beta\). Use the actual localized sheaf isomorphism to a sum of dihedral models. Its single edge component carries both \(I\) and \(J\) into the same model edge coordinates, because it commutes with both endpoint restrictions.

On a model summand containing \(y\), the lower and upper kernels are generated by products of the corresponding remaining supported labels. After reduction modulo \(\alpha\), every surviving label in this plane is a unit times the same reduced linear factor. If \(h_x,h_y\) count those factors, the two image ideals have orders \(h_x,h_y\).

The transported later-edge count gives \(h_x-h_y=k_P\), the exponent contributed by this plane to \(f_e\). Other plane factors become units. Thus the lower model image equals \(f_e\) times the upper model image in these coordinates. If the model does not contain \(y\), its edge module is zero and the equality is automatic. Taking the finite sum and transporting through the same edge isomorphism gives localized membership of \(z\) in \(S\).

### Step 4. Check every prime needed for denominator removal

Every irreducible divisor of the reduction of \(f_eg\) in \(C\) is associated with a reduced root factor: \(f_e\) is a product of such factors, and so is \(g\). For such a factor represented by \(\beta\), Step 3 gives a scalar \(s\) outside the plane prime with \(sz\in S\). Its reduction is not divisible by that irreducible factor.

For any irreducible not dividing \(f_eg\), the denominator from Step 2 itself already has the required nondivisibility. Hence prime-local membership is available for every irreducible of \(C\).

### Step 5. Remove denominators in the free target

Apply [[node:kl.local.free_target_localization]] inside the torsion-free \(C\)-module \(B^e\), with the free target \(S\) and the generic denominator from Step 2. Its coordinatewise prime-cancellation argument gives \(z\in S\). Since \(z\in I\) was arbitrary, \(I\subseteq f_eJ\).

### Step 6. Match the current schedule's condition sets

For a suffix \(e::q\), the remaining outgoing conditions at each endpoint are the strictly later labels relative to the transported label of \(e\). Equal labels do not meet those endpoints. Restricting the fixed ambient sheaf to the current interval retains all outgoing edges from its vertices and the same scores. Thus the ambient inclusion just proved is exactly the scheduled inclusion in the statement.

The degree formula for \(f_e\) is the separate inversion-plane calculation. No freeness of the current lower kernel, and no equality \(I=f_eJ\), was assumed.
