# Rank-one structure sheaves on dihedral intervals

On a relative dihedral graph, fix a top \(c\). Its structure model \(\mathcal O_c\) has stalk \(A\) at each \(x\le_D c\) and zero outside that lower ideal. An edge supported below \(c\) has module \(A/(\alpha_e)\), with the endpoint maps given by reduction modulo its label.

A section is therefore a collection of polynomials satisfying the edge congruences
\[
f_x\equiv f_y\pmod{\alpha_e}.
\]
The model is generated by sections and is flabby for relative upper sets. Its edge quotient rule is literal. In the dihedral Coxeter system, every comparable pair also has \(P^D_{x,c}=1\), explaining the rank-one stalks in the paper's description.

For a supported vertex and a set \(F\) of its outgoing supported edges, the kernel conditions say that its polynomial is divisible by every \(\alpha_e\), \(e\in F\). Distinct incident reflections have nonproportional linear labels. Since the coefficient ring is a unique factorization domain,
\[
L_x(F)=\left(\prod_{e\in F}\alpha_e\right)A.
\]
This uses coprimality of the linear factors, not comaximality of their ideals.

After root-plane localization and reduction by one edge label, the remaining plane labels are uniformizers times units. The valuation of this product becomes the number of remaining supported edges. This is the precise passage from dihedral combinatorics to the local comparison of sheaf images.

The polynomial identity \(P^D=1\), the induced-label model properties, and the local decomposition are distinct steps in the development; each is retained at its own source interface.
