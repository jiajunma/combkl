# The plane prime and its local coefficient ring

Fix a finite-generator Coxeter system. Let \(V_0\) be its original real root space, \(V\) its extended realization, and \(i_0:V_0\hookrightarrow V\) the embedding. Choose a finite basis of \(V\), giving
\[
\Phi:\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathbb R}(V)\xrightarrow{\sim}A,
\qquad A=\mathbb R[X_1,\ldots,X_N],
\]
and the linear-label map
\[
\lambda:V_0\to A,\qquad
\lambda(v)=\Phi(\iota(i_0v)).
\]

## The prime generated by a subspace

For any real subspace \(P\subseteq V_0\), define
\[
\mathfrak p_P=(\lambda(v):v\in P)\subseteq A.
\]
This is planePrime. In the source it is the ideal map under \(\Phi\) of the symmetric-algebra ideal generated by \(i_0(P)\).

The [[node:kl.detail.linear_quotient|linear-quotient theorem]] gives
\[
A/\mathfrak p_P\simeq
\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathbb R}(V/i_0(P)).
\]
The right side is a polynomial algebra over \(\mathbb R\), hence a domain. Therefore \(\mathfrak p_P\) is prime.

For positive roots \(a,b\) and \(P=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{a,b\}\),
\[
\mathfrak p_P=(\lambda(a),\lambda(b)).
\]
Linearity expresses the label of every vector in \(P\) as a real linear combination of these two labels. The [[node:kl.local.plane_prime_membership|plane-prime membership theorem]] records this specialization and the exact linear membership criterion.

## The local coefficient ring

Define
\[
R_P=A_{\mathfrak p_P}
=(A\setminus\mathfrak p_P)^{-1}A,
\qquad \eta_P:A\to R_P,\quad f\mapsto f/1.
\]
This is PlaneRing. Its elements are fractions \(f/s\), with \(s\notin\mathfrak p_P\). Since \(A\) is a domain, equality of fractions is checked by cross multiplication.

The nonunits are exactly the fractions whose numerator lies in \(\mathfrak p_P\). If \(f\notin\mathfrak p_P\), then \(s/f\) is the inverse of \(f/s\). If \(f\in\mathfrak p_P\) and \((f/s)(g/t)=1\), cross multiplication would give \(fg=st\). The left side lies in the prime ideal and the right side does not, a contradiction.

Fractions with numerator in \(\mathfrak p_P\) form a proper ideal: addition has numerator \(ft+gs\) when the two fractions are \(f/s,g/t\), and multiplication preserves membership in the prime ideal. Every element outside this ideal is a unit, so a proper ideal can contain only elements of it. It is therefore the unique maximal ideal
\[
\mathfrak m_P=\mathfrak p_PR_P
=\{f/s:f\in\mathfrak p_P,\ s\notin\mathfrak p_P\}.
\]
Thus \(R_P\) is a local ring. The geometric applications choose a two-dimensional root plane for \(P\).

## Localized linear labels

The **localized linear-label map** is
\[
\lambda_P:V_0\to R_P,\qquad
\lambda_P(v)=\eta_P(\lambda(v)).
\]
This is localizedLinear and is real-linear. The [[node:kl.local.plane_prime_membership|plane-prime membership theorem]] gives
\[
\lambda_P(v)\in\mathfrak m_P\Longleftrightarrow v\in P,
\qquad
\lambda_P(v)\text{ a unit}\Longleftrightarrow v\notin P.
\]

In the illustrative ring \(A=\mathbb R[\alpha,\beta,\gamma]\) with
\(\mathfrak p_P=(\alpha,\beta)\), both \(\gamma\) and \(1+\alpha\) become units. The elements \(\alpha,\beta\) lie in the maximal ideal.

Setting one localized root label equal to zero produces the localized edge ring, defined separately together with its reduction map.
