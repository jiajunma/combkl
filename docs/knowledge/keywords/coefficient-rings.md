A basis of the extended realization gives the polynomial coefficient algebra \(A\). A positive root determines a degree-one polynomial \(\lambda(a)\). The edge coefficient ring is the quotient by this polynomial, and the actual edge module carries that quotient action through the upper-quotient rule.

A root plane determines a linear ideal and its localization. Labels outside the plane become units. After quotienting by the current root label, any other independent root in the plane reduces to a scalar associate of a chosen reference root. This is the coefficient-ring calculation behind the local product comparison.

The quotient and localization maps must be followed together: the product factors, the correction scalar and both edge images all act in the same localized edge module. [[node:kl.local.coset_edge_reduction|Root-label reduction]] identifies their actual coefficient rings before the later-edge counts compare their powers. The sequence below introduces the rings and then the maps used by that comparison.
