---
title: Finite intervals and their transported structure
primary_topic: kl.coxeter
---

An interval \([u,b]\) is a finite collection of vertices with the induced Bruhat order. Its intrinsic rank difference is measured by Coxeter length. Order isomorphisms identify the bottom and top and preserve these relative ranks, which makes interval data suitable for induction.

The proof also uses relative intervals inside a root-plane subgroup coset. Their ranks are measured in the induced dihedral system. The [[node:kl.notes.relative_dihedral_transport|full interval transport]] theorem constructs target coset coordinates, proves affine preservation of relative lengths, and shows that the image fills the entire target relative interval. Its full-range statement permits exact reindexing of the later-edge sums.

At the end of the proof, [[node:kl.main.finite_model_transport|finite-support reduction]] realizes an interval inside a Coxeter system generated by finitely many simple reflections and transports the canonical polynomial with it. Thus interval finiteness has two roles: it supports finite sheaf and schedule constructions, and it makes the unrestricted theorem accessible through finite models.
