title: Sheaf operations that retain the actual kernel and image maps

Restriction, pullback and scalar extension change the setting of a sheaf while keeping precise maps between its modules. Restriction to an upper interval retains the relevant outgoing conditions. Pullback along a graph map identifies selected equations when every surviving outgoing edge has a lift. Scalar extension replaces modules by tensor products and carries their restriction maps along.

The finite kernel-and-image results are central to localization. Flat scalar extension preserves the embedded selected kernels and their lower and upper images. After root-plane localization, edge modules with unit labels vanish; the remaining equations are exactly those represented on the relative coset graph.

These results make the local model calculation an equality about the original localized boundary sheaf. Both endpoint images are transported through the same edge module, and the correction scalar keeps its specified coefficient action. The reading route follows the operations first and then their actual kernel and image identities.
