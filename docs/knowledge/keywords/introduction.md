The central objects are a Coxeter group, one of its Bruhat intervals, and the canonical KL polynomial attached to the interval endpoints. Begin with words and length, then the definition of Bruhat order. The polynomial side begins with explicit Hecke operators; the quadratic relation and bar involution determine the coefficient families that will be compared.

The proof connects these objects through a boundary sheaf on the reflection graph. A stalk is a graded module, an edge module is a quotient by a root label, and selected kernels record the outgoing restrictions that remain to be imposed. Reading these definitions makes the later image comparisons concrete: both images are submodules of one edge module.

The [[node:kl.local.edge_inclusion|global edge inclusion]] and [[node:kl.comparison.edge_increment|one-edge Hilbert comparison]] form the central calculation. They feed a sequence of elementary transfers, whose endpoints are actual sheaf characters. The final induction uses exactness of those endpoints to recover freeness and polynomial invariance together. The reading sequence selects a first pass through this chain; the keyword and topic pages then expose its detailed prerequisites.
