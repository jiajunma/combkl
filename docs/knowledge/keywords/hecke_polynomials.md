The normalized R-family is defined from Hecke bar coefficients. Its diagonal value, vanishing range and simple-descent recursion give the computational structure used later by the path construction. The square-parameter conversion relates this family to ordinary R-polynomials.

In the combinatorial invariance proof, the R-family is the first polynomial object compared across intervals. Ordered reflection paths describe its matrix entries for the geometric orders constructed in the source. The Hilbert comparison produces inequalities involving that transported matrix, and simultaneous induction turns those inequalities into equality of R-polynomials.

The canonical KL family is then treated through its reciprocity relation and degree bound. Once the R-data agree, uniqueness eliminates the remaining difference of KL polynomials. This keyword therefore follows the two polynomial layers in their proof order: bar coefficients and R-recursion first, canonical KL identification afterward.
