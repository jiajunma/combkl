The Hecke construction begins with a standard module whose basis is indexed by group elements. Left and right simple operators use the length comparison with a simple reflection. Their quadratic and Coxeter relations make the operator calculus compatible with multiplication.

The quadratic relation gives an explicit inverse for each simple operator. Together with the word and braid arguments, this supports the inverse-basis transform and bar involution. The coefficients of the bar transform define the normalized R-family, whose descent recursion becomes the polynomial recursion used in the invariance proof.

[[node:kl.foundations.canonical_kl|Canonical KL uniqueness]] is the endpoint of this algebraic development: normalization, reciprocity and the degree condition determine the canonical families. The main proof first establishes invariance of R-data and then applies that uniqueness to obtain KL invariance. Reading the operator definitions and their normalizations first makes these two polynomial stages distinct and precise.
