The image comparison uses actual submodules and scalar actions. A module killed by a scalar acquires an action of the quotient coefficient ring. An existing submodule then keeps its vectors while its scalar stability is expressed over that quotient. Saturation identifies the image of a selected upper kernel with the quotient of that kernel by the current label.

Two kinds of module argument are used later. An injective scalar map transports a free basis to the corrected upper image. Prime-denominator membership then proves containment in this free target by removing prime factors from a multiplier. In the reverse direction, two presentations of one image yield a projective kernel through an explicit splitting.

The hypotheses belong to the particular module and coefficient ring at each stage. The selected upper kernel is free over the polynomial algebra; its edge image and corrected target are free over the edge quotient. These constructions connect the local image calculation to the global inclusion and then to the recovery of free kernels.
