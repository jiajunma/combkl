The realization provides vectors for roots; the coefficient algebra provides degree-one polynomials representing those vectors. A finite basis of the extended realization identifies its symmetric algebra with a polynomial ring. Each root then supplies a linear polynomial label on the corresponding reflection edge.

The linear-label theorems prove the algebraic properties used by sheaves: nonzero root labels are prime, independent roots give distinct linear directions, and quotienting by the current label produces the edge coefficient ring. A plane spanned by roots similarly determines an ideal and a localization.

Grading records the degrees of these constructions. The root labels have degree one, so a regular scalar quotient contributes the factor \(1-q\) to a Hilbert value. The number of polynomial variables is the cardinality of the actual coefficient basis. This connection between geometry, coefficients and grading leads directly to the local image and normalized Hilbert comparisons.
