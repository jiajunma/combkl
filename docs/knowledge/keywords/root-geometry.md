title: Root signs, planes and geometric weights
primary_topic: kl.reflection_combinatorics

In the finite-generator realization, positive roots give canonical labels for reflections. Their inversion signs detect whether left multiplication lowers length. A root plane collects the roots lying in the span of two independent positive roots; its induced subgroup carries a dihedral Coxeter system with its own length.

The geometric weight of a plane measures its contribution to the excess length of a reflection edge. The construction pairs positive root lines under the current reflection, chooses representatives of the contributing pairs, and groups their contributions by the plane they span with the current root. The plane inversion-change theorem relates each contribution to an intrinsic dihedral length difference.

These weights determine the correction factor used in the edge-image comparison. Their sum gives its global degree, while each individual weight becomes the exponent of one reduced root at the corresponding plane localization. The chain from inversion signs to local exponents explains why rank-two geometry controls the later module calculation.
